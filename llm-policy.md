<header class="page-header">
  <h1>On LLMs</h1>
  <p class="lede">a note about how the notes get written, because i’d want to know.</p>
</header>

<div class="post-content">
  <p>everything here is mine. the ideas, the opinions, the arguments, the things i turn out to be wrong about. all of it started in my head and stayed there through however many drafts it took.</p>

  <p>a language model is sometimes involved in what happens after that. mostly a local one, running on my own machine rather than somebody else’s. specifically, it may:</p>

  <ul>
    <li>rephrase a sentence i couldn’t get right on my own</li>
    <li>fix grammar, punctuation and the typos i stop being able to see after the third read</li>
    <li>formalise something i wrote too casually, or loosen something i wrote too stiffly</li>
    <li>talk me out of saying something that will get me in trouble, or cut a sentence that came out stronger than i actually meant it</li>
    <li>handle the mechanical parts of publishing: files, formatting, the site itself</li>
  </ul>

  <p>what it does not do is decide what i think. if a note argues something, i am the one arguing it. if a note is wrong, i am the one who is wrong. nothing here is a model’s opinion wearing my name, and nothing here went up without me having read every word of it and agreed to it.</p>

  <p>i don’t think using a language model this way requires an apology. i do think it requires saying so.</p>
</div>
