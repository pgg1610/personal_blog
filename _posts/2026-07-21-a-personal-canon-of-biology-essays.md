These are essays and papers I return to when I want to think more clearly about biology, biomedical research, and scientific progress. They differ in form and ambition, but each has shaped my taste as a reader and writer.

This is not a definitive syllabus. It is a personal canon, and an evolving one.

1. [Diagnosing the decline in pharmaceutical R&D efficiency](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrd3681), Jack W. Scannell et al. (2012)
2. [Predictive validity in drug discovery: what it is, why it matters and how to improve it](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-022-00439-z), Jack W. Scannell et al. (2022)
3. [Is the cell really a machine?](https://aeon.co/essays/are-cells-machines), Daniel J. Nicholson (2019)
4. [How academia and publishing are destroying scientific innovation](https://www.theatlantic.com/science/archive/2014/12/how-academia-and-publishing-are-destroying-scientific-innovation-a-conversation-with-sydney-brenner/383382/), a conversation with Sydney Brenner (2014)
5. [A Future History of Biomedical Progress](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/a-future-history-of-biomedical-progress), Adam Green (2022)
6. [The pharma industry from Paul Janssen to today](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-pharma-industry-from-paul-janssen), Alex Telford (2023)
7. *The Lives of a Cell*, Lewis Thomas (1974)
8. [The maddening saga of how an Alzheimer's 'cabal' thwarted progress toward a cure for decades](https://www.statnews.com/2019/06/25/alzheimers-cabal-thwarted-progress-toward-cure/), Sharon Begley (2019)
9. [The Scientific Virtues](https://slimemoldtimemold.com/2022/02/10/the-scientific-virtues/), Slime Mold Time Mold (2022)
10. [First Clean Water, Now Clean Air](https://www.finmoorhouse.com/p/first-clean-water-now-clean-air), Fin Moorhouse (2023)
11. [I should have loved biology](https://jsomers.net/i-should-have-loved-biology/), James Somers (2020)
12. [The Baffling Intelligence of a Single Cell](https://nautil.us/the-baffling-intelligence-of-a-single-cell-235850/), James Somers and Edwin Morris (2024)
13. [Biology is more theoretical than physics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z0Q8rQH3GE0), Jeremy Gunawardena (2013)
14. [Can a biologist fix a radio?](https://www.cell.com/cancer-cell/fulltext/S1535-6108(02)00133-2), Yuri Lazebnik (2002)
15. [Cells are very fast and crowded places](https://www.righto.com/2011/11/cells-are-very-fast-and-crowded.html), Ken Shirriff (2011)
16. [Life at Low Reynolds Number](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/gold/pdfs/purcell.pdf), E. M. Purcell (1977)
17. [Lena](https://qntm.org/lena), qntm (2021)
18. *Sequences and Consequences*, Sydney Brenner (2010)
19. [The NIH Report](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-nih-report), Matt Faherty (2022)
20. [Simplicity in biology](https://www.nature.com/articles/445873a), Uri Alon (2007)
21. [A breakthrough from 60 years ago: “General nature of the genetic code for proteins”](https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/view/MS-ADD-09669-00005-00006/1), Matthew Cobb (2021)
22. [Molecular Vitalism](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(00)81529-2), Marc Kirschner, John Gerhart, and Tim Mitchison (2000)
23. [The Coming Technological Singularity](https://edoras.sdsu.edu/~vinge/misc/singularity.html), Vernor Vinge (1993)
24. [Review of Scientific Self-Experimentation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6066824/), Brian Hanley, William Bains, and George Church (2018)
25. [Coming full circle—from endless complexity to simplicity and back again](https://www.cell.com/cancer-cell/fulltext/S1535-6108(14)00082-4), Robert Weinberg (2014)
26. [Nothing in Biology Makes Sense Except in the Light of Evolution](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1293229), Theodosius Dobzhansky (1973)
27. [The Impersonator: The Fake Data Were Coming From Inside the Lab](https://www.science.org/content/article/fake-data-come-inside-lab), Uri Simonsohn (2024)
28. [The Longevity FAQ](https://nintil.com/longevity-faq), José Luis Ricón (2020)
29. [The Perfect Human Is Puerto Rican](https://liorpachter.wordpress.com/2014/12/07/the-perfect-human-is-puerto-rican/), Lior Pachter (2014)
30. [No Evidence of Disease](https://worksinprogress.co/issue/no-evidence-of-disease/), Stephanie Bourque (2012)

If you read one of these, I would start with Scannell on R&D efficiency, Lazebnik's radio essay, Somers on learning to love biology, and Thomas's *The Lives of a Cell*.
