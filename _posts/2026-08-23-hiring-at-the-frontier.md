I’ve been building Frontier AI (FAI) at Lilly for the past 18 months now. Frontier AI is a boutique team meant to rethink how we do molecule discovery at Lilly. We started by build out Kernel Lilly, our first research agent system since 2024, just when tool-use LLMs were starting to take off. It was clear that besides base models being good, the need for connectors and runtime environments for the models are going to be key (as cool kids now call it the harness). Since then we expanded FAI from pure agent builders to include agent harness design and started to build capabilities to connect the agent with our research harness to key laboratory automation platforms (agentic labs).

Building FAI from scratch has been a fun experience. Hiring at the frontier of AI and life science especially in late 2024 when agentic AI was in its infancy was uncharter territory. It took a lot of rethinking and recaliberating of how hiring and talent identification happens. Everyone who is now part of FAI has been selected meticulously by me with help of my collaborators who are also end-users of the sysystems we build. 

Few weeks into hiring we quickly realized that the conventional way of interview suck and are not meant for the comming AI-age. Coding interviews can be gamed and “culture” round can be faked. Graham Duncan has [writen](https://grahamduncan.blog/whats-going-on-here/) beautifully about hiring and his experience in building teams. It inspired a lot of our interview and hiring decisions. Through our interview process the hope was to reveal the character of the person, the way they think and operate, and if FAI is the right place for them.

As we continue to hire, I wanted to share few things that I personally look at in the candidates:

1. **Integrity.** I place huge value on this virtue, more so than grades or technical competency. If a candidate is willing to say things to get them hired it is an automantic reject for me. That does not mean you need to know everything but self-awareness and not being afraid to look stupid is important.  
2. **Passion.** You don’t need to fit a cookie-cutter mold of computational chemistry or bioinformatics, or more recently AI engineering. When I came to Lilly I had never seen a protein structure before. I am looking for passion, a flame of genius that can be stoked. We are hiring for the thinking process than what you thought about. If you can explain an idea you are passionate about simply. Share how you implemented it and why did you make those decisions, who helped you along thew way? A simple clear example devoid of any buzz words is a good signal that you are really passionate about it. 
3. **Relentlessly resourceful.** It follows passion closely. People can be passionate but arm-chair philosophers. Are you going out of your comfort zone to bring about a change? How do you learn and incorporate feedback? Ideas are cheap, execution is expensive. At FAI, no ask is too small or too big, keeping personal bandwidth in check, you are expected to wear multiple hats working at the frontier of what's possible. This does not mean you vibe code and except some one to make it engineerable, having the discipline to understand the spectrum of prototype to production is important. This also dovetails in the ability to communicate what did not work but having a bias to action to try different ideas quickly with clear intention behind each. 

Lead by example of your work. Ask good questions. Be genuinely curious. A near perfect team is made up smart, passionate, imperfect people who complement each other. If these ideas and the mission moves you, please apply and I look forward to chatting soon!
